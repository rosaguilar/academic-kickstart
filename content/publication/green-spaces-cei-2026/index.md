abstract: "Rapid urbanization contributes to significant land use and land cover changes, including the decline of urban green spaces (UGS) in cities worldwide. Public urban green spaces (PUGS) are particularly important because they provide accessible recreational areas supporting environmental quality, biodiversity, and human well-being. Ensuring equitable access to green and public spaces, addressed in SDG 11.7, requires reliable and up-to-date information on PUGS. Traditional mapping approaches based on field surveys and manual interpretation are often costly, time-consuming, and difficult to maintain in rapidly changing urban environments. Recent advances in deep learning and the increasing availability of remote sensing data open opportunities for automated UGS mapping. However, distinguishing PUGS remains challenging because public accessibility cannot be directly inferred from satellite imagery alone. Previous studies demonstrated the value of integrating OpenStreetMap (OSM) data with remote sensing information, yet existing approaches often rely on proprietary software, limiting reproducibility and wider adoption. This study addresses this gap by developing a fully reproducible PUGS mapping workflow based exclusively on publicly available Sentinel-2 imagery, OSM data, and open-source Python libraries. The workflow covers data acquisition, preprocessing, deep learning model development, and evaluation. Specifically, the study investigates: (1) which additional information derived from OSM can support PUGS mapping, (2) how the integration of OSM-derived information influences model performance, and (3) how detection performance varies across different PUGS dimensional categories. The proposed workflow aims to support the creation and maintenance of up-to-date PUGS inventories and facilitate broader adoption of reproducible PUGS mapping approaches by researchers, planners, and policymakers."
